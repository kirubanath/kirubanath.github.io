02

Things agents taught me, mostly the hard way

Field notes. Each one cost at least one bad afternoon.

A judge from the same family is a character witness.

I built a judge to check an agent's code before it ran. It did not help, because it was the same model family as the agent it was judging. On a different family, it worked.

You cannot talk to every model the same way.

GPT models did best with the context first and the instructions after. Claude models followed an instruction better when it came with the reason. I know people like both.

Sometimes the right instruction is "check your work less".

We had about 24 hours per problem. Lighter validation meant a slightly noisier score and many more experiments. More ideas were worth more than steadier scores.

A long run has many ways to die.

Rate limits, mostly. So every model call went through one function that retries and waits longer each time. A limit should slow a run down, not end it.

Decide what the model may get wrong.

Wording can be a little off. A number cannot. So the model writes the words, the numbers come from verified data, and a person decides anything that matters.

If you cannot look at it afterwards, do not ship it.

Trace every model call. When an output reads wrong, you want to see what the model was given and what it wrote. The alternative is a séance.

04

Two true stories

For when you want to know what I am like when the plan fails. Which it does.

The paper nobody planned

Accepted at a conference we never meant to submit to

PiML was not written for AutoML. We wrote it for another conference, which said no. AutoML asked for something different, so we ran new experiments overnight, made new figures and rewrote the whole paper in two days. It was accepted.

I do not recommend this as a process. It is also my most successful process.

JFK

New York, with no phone

I flew to New York to present that paper. I landed at JFK alone, with an international eSIM ready, and found out my phone did not support US network bands. At all. I had read that the premium cabs carry wifi, so I booked one, asked the driver to share his connection, and got to my hotel. Then I ordered a phone that worked, and spent two days getting around New York on public wifi until it arrived.

I build systems that recover from failure. Sometimes I am the system.
